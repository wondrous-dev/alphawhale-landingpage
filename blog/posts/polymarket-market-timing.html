Why Timing Matters

When you enter and exit positions significantly impacts your returns. Good timing can turn a good idea into a great trade, while poor timing can turn a good idea into a loss.

Timing affects entry price, exit price, holding period, and opportunity cost. Mastering timing improves execution and profitability.

Related: Polymarket Exit Strategies: When and How to Close Positions

Entry Timing Strategies

When to enter positions:

Early entry: Enter before information becomes widely known. News timing: Enter before scheduled news events. Technical timing: Enter on chart patterns or indicators. Value timing: Enter when price is favorable relative to probability. Momentum timing: Enter when momentum is building.

Exit Timing Strategies

When to exit positions:

Profit targets: Exit when reaching predetermined profit levels. Time-based: Exit after holding for target period. Resolution proximity: Exit as resolution date approaches. News events: Exit before or after major events. Technical signals: Exit on chart patterns or indicators.

Pre-News Positioning

Entering before news breaks:

Anticipate events: Research what's expected and position accordingly. Build gradually: Enter positions before news to avoid premium prices. Use limit orders: Set orders at target prices before news. Monitor information flow: Track sources that might break news early. Risk management: Size positions appropriately for pre-news uncertainty.

News Event Timing

Trading around news:

Before news: Enter if you have strong conviction about likely outcomes. During news: React quickly when news breaks (requires speed). After news: Enter after initial volatility settles. Fade moves: Counter-trade if initial reactions seem overdone. Post-news analysis: Trade on implications that become clear later.

Time-of-Day Patterns

Intraday timing considerations:

Market hours: Some markets are more active during specific hours. Liquidity patterns: Trade when liquidity is highest. News timing: Major news often breaks during business hours. Global markets: Different time zones affect activity. Weekend patterns: Activity may decrease on weekends.

Day-of-Week Patterns

Weekly timing patterns:

Monday activity: Markets may be more active after weekends. Mid-week: Often highest activity periods. Friday: Activity may decrease before weekends. Event days: Specific days with scheduled events. Historical patterns: Learn patterns for your focus markets.

Resolution Proximity Timing

Timing relative to resolution:

Far from resolution: More time for thesis to play out. Approaching resolution: Prices tend to converge toward outcomes. Final days: High volatility and rapid price movements. Exit timing: Consider exiting before final volatility. Opportunity cost: Don't tie up capital unnecessarily long.

Market Cycle Timing

Understanding market cycles:

Market creation: New markets may price inefficiently initially. Maturation: Markets become more efficient over time. News cycles: Markets move through news-driven cycles. Resolution convergence: Prices move toward $0 or $1 as resolution nears. Post-resolution: Markets close after resolution.

Technical Timing

Using charts for timing:

Support/resistance: Enter near support, exit near resistance. Breakouts: Enter on breakouts, exit on breakdowns. Trend following: Enter in direction of trend. Reversals: Enter on reversal signals. Indicators: Use technical indicators for timing signals.

Fundamental Timing

Timing based on information:

Information advantage: Enter when you have information others don't. Information diffusion: Enter before information becomes widely known. Catalyst timing: Enter before expected catalysts. Thesis timing: Enter when thesis becomes more likely. Exit timing: Exit when information is fully priced in.

Liquidity Timing

Trading when liquidity is best:

High volume periods: Trade when volume is highest. Active hours: Trade during most active trading hours. Avoid thin periods: Don't trade when liquidity is low. Spread awareness: Trade when spreads are tightest. Execution quality: Better execution during high liquidity.

Event-Driven Timing

Timing around specific events:

Elections: Enter before polls, exit after results. Earnings: Enter before announcements, exit after. Debates: Enter before, exit after or during. Data releases: Enter before, exit after data. Policy decisions: Enter before, exit after decisions.

Common Timing Mistakes

Errors to avoid:

Chasing moves: Entering after prices have already moved significantly. Too early: Entering before catalysts materialize. Too late: Entering after information is fully priced in. Ignoring timing: Not considering when to enter/exit. Emotional timing: Letting emotions drive timing decisions.

Timing Tools

Resources to help:

Calendars: Know when events are scheduled. Alert systems: Notifications for news or price movements. Charting tools: Technical analysis for timing. News feeds: Fast news sources for timing. Analytics: Tools that analyze timing patterns.

Building Timing Skills

Developing timing ability:

Study patterns: Learn timing patterns for your focus markets. Practice: Paper trade to practice timing. Track results: Monitor how timing affects outcomes. Learn from mistakes: Analyze timing errors. Refine approach: Improve timing based on experience.

Timing and Risk

Balancing timing with risk:

Early entry risk: Entering early means more uncertainty. Late entry risk: Entering late means missing moves. Timing vs. certainty: Trade-off between timing and conviction. Risk management: Adjust position size based on timing uncertainty. Diversification: Spread timing across multiple positions.

Timing Examples

Real-world scenarios:

Election trader: Enter 2-4 weeks before election, exit day before or after. News trader: Enter minutes before news, exit minutes after. Value trader: Enter when price is favorable, exit when thesis plays out. Technical trader: Enter on signals, exit on signals. Long-term trader: Enter early, hold until resolution.

Timing Framework

Building your approach:

Define timing rules: Clear rules for when to enter/exit. Consider factors: News, technical, fundamental, liquidity. Set priorities: Which timing factors matter most. Test approach: Validate timing strategy. Refine: Improve based on results.

Best Practices

Timing guidelines:

Plan ahead: Know when you'll enter/exit before trading. Be flexible: Adjust timing when conditions change. Stay disciplined: Follow your timing rules. Monitor conditions: Stay aware of timing factors. Review regularly: Assess and improve timing.

Market timing significantly impacts trading results. Develop timing strategies, use tools to identify timing opportunities, and continuously refine your approach. Good timing improves entry and exit prices, making it an essential skill for successful trading.
